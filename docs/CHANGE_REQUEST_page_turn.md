## TASK 2 — Remove the watermarks from panel images

Several images in `public/images/comics/` still have the generator's watermark in a corner. Fix every image.

### 1. Audit

For every file in `public/images/comics/`:
- Open it and inspect it (Read the image).
- Record in `review/watermark-audit.md`:
  - which corner the mark is in;
  - its approximate size in pixels;
  - whether important content (hardware, the focal point) lies within about 12% of that corner.

### 2. Fix: reframe each image (preferred)

- **Crop.** Write `scripts/assets/reframe.py` (Pillow, run with `python3 -I`). It crops each image to remove the watermarked corner, keeping the 3:2 aspect ratio:
  - trim the bottom and right (or the affected sides) by the watermark's size plus a 2% margin;
  - then crop the opposite sides proportionally, so the focal point stays centered.
- **Resize and export.** Resize back to 1536×1024 with Lanczos, then export as both:
  - WebP at quality 82;
  - JPG at quality 80 as a fallback.
- **Keep the originals.** Move them to `assets-src/comics-original/`, which stays out of the build and in git, and write the cleaned files to `public/images/comics/`.

### 3. If cropping can't work

If cropping would cut important content (for example, the rover sits in the watermarked corner):
- do NOT invent new scene content in the image;
- list the file in `review/watermark-audit.md` under **"REGENERATE"**, with the panel's prompt from `ASSET_PROMPTS.md`, so the team can regenerate it;
- until then, the UI covers that corner with the `ILLUSTRATION` label plate, which sits exactly over the old mark.

### 4. Acceptance criteria

- Look at every cleaned image at 100% zoom. No watermark is visible at any size.
- Responsive `object-fit` cropping doesn't bring a watermark back at any viewport.

## TASK 3 — 3D page-turning comic reader

Replace the current panel stepper in State 2 (Interactive Narrative) with a book you physically page through. The page turn is the signature interaction of the site, so it has to feel tactile, not like a CSS card flip.

### Architecture: a hybrid of WebGL turning and DOM at rest

**While a page is turning:** pages are WebGL meshes inside the existing R3F canvas.
- Do not add a second always-on canvas. Reuse the app's canvas, using drei `<View>` scissoring if the layout needs it.
- Each page is a `SkinnedMesh`:
  - a thin `BoxGeometry` (paper thickness about 0.003 units), subdivided along its width into about 30 segments;
  - a chain of about 30 bones along the width.
- Turning rotates the bones with a curl falloff:
  - stronger rotation near the spine at the start of the turn;
  - the free edge lags behind, then catches up.
  - This produces a real paper bend, not a rigid hinge.
- The front and back faces use textures made from that page's panel composition, rendered once to an offscreen canvas/texture (`html-to-image` or a 2D-canvas compositor) and cached.
  - Generate textures at about 2048 px on the long edge for desktop, 1024 px on mobile.
- **Lighting:**
  - soft key light;
  - the turning page casts a moving shadow onto the page underneath (a shadow-only plane or a shader gradient);
  - a subtle specular sheen sweeps across the paper as it bends;
  - slight paper roughness and a cream page edge visible on the thickness.

**While a page is at rest:** show crisp DOM content, positioned exactly over the resting page mesh:
- caption boxes, speech balloons, hotspots, the illustration/archival toggle;
- a screen-reader-friendly reading order.

**Swap rule:**
- When a turn starts: DOM fades out (80 ms) and the mesh takes over.
- When the turn ends: the mesh is replaced by the DOM for the new spread, with no visible jump.
- Verify alignment with screenshots: zero layout shift at the swap.

### Book structure (per mission, from `missions.json`)

1. **Cover:** issue number, title, location, coordinates, mission palette.
2. **Spreads:** two pages each on desktop, one on mobile. Panels are laid out with the existing comic panel grammar: irregular frames, gutters, bleeds.
3. **Back cover:** "Inspect the hardware →". This hands off to State 3 with a camera move (see Task 4.5).

### Interactions (all must work)

| Input | Behavior |
|---|---|
| Drag a page corner or edge (mouse/touch) | The page follows the pointer; the curl amount follows the drag distance. Release past 40% (or a flick faster than ~0.5 px/ms) completes the turn; otherwise the page springs back. |
| Click or tap the right/left page edge | Animated turn forward/back |
| `→` `←`, `PageDown` `PageUp`, `Space` | Turn forward/back |
| Swipe on mobile | Turn (single-page mode) |
| Progress scrubber | Jump to a spread (fast multi-page riffle, max ~600 ms total) |

### Motion and feel

- **Timing:** about 900 ms for an animated turn, eased with a custom curve (fast lift, slow settle). Add the duration and easing to the shared motion tokens.
- **Sound:** a quiet paper-turn sound through `src/lib/audio.ts`, respecting mute. Two or three variants picked at random so it doesn't repeat.

### State

- Add `narrative.spreadIndex` and `narrative.turning` to the Zustand store.
- Deep-link to a spread with `?issue=01&page=3`.

### Fallbacks

- **`prefers-reduced-motion`:** no curl. Do a 200 ms crossfade plus a slight horizontal slide.
- **No WebGL, or low-power mobile:** a CSS 3D fallback (`transform-style: preserve-3d`, `rotateY` on the page, a gradient shadow), still draggable.
- **Missing panel image:** the archival photo or a styled placeholder panel. Never a broken image.

### Performance

- Only the current spread's pages ±1 have live meshes and textures. Dispose of the others: geometry, material and texture.
- At 4× CPU throttle on desktop, page turns hold ≥55 FPS.
- No GPU memory growth after paging through all three books twice. Check `renderer.info`.

### Accessibility

- The book is a region labeled "Issue 01 comic, page 3 of 8".
- Turning announces the new page through an `aria-live="polite"` region.
- Every caption and balloon is real DOM text in reading order.
- The illustration/archival toggle is a real `<button aria-pressed>`.

## TASK 4 — Immersive details embedded in the pages

Panels should feel alive and explorable inside the book, not like flat pictures. Pick these per panel through new optional fields in `missions.json`. Do not apply everything everywhere: about 1–2 immersive elements per spread.

Extend the panel schema (TypeScript interface plus JSON):

```ts
interface ComicPanel {
  id: string;
  image: string;
  imageKind: 'illustration' | 'archival';
  archival?: { src: string; credit: string; sourceUrl: string };
  caption?: string;
  dialogue?: { speaker: string; text: string }[];
  depth?: { layers?: string[]; depthMap?: string; strength?: number };
  insert?: { type: 'model'; model: string; node?: string; cameraPreset: string; caption: string };
  hotspots?: { x: number; y: number; label: string; fact: string; source: string }[];
  ambient?: 'dust' | 'static' | 'laser' | 'rotor-blur' | 'stars' | null;
}
```

### 4.1 Depth parallax ("2.5D" panels)

- Panels with `depth.depthMap` render in a small shader plane: a parallax-occlusion-style displacement driven by the pointer or device tilt, with subtle strength (about 6–12 px of travel).
- Create depth maps offline. Write `scripts/assets/depth.py`, using Depth Anything V2 Small if available locally; if not, document the command.
- Alternatively, use hand-cut layers (`depth.layers`: background, midground, foreground PNGs).
- Disabled under reduced motion.

### 4.2 Embedded 3D inserts

- A panel with `insert` shows a live, small, rotatable view of the real mission model inside the panel frame, as a "pop-out" detail. For example:
  - Opportunity's arm turret next to the blueberry panel;
  - the retroreflector next to the Apollo experiments panel;
  - the broken rotor blade on Flight 72.
- Use drei `<View>` tied to the panel's DOM rect (one shared canvas) and the already-loaded GLB. Frame the named node with `cameraPreset`.
- Idle auto-rotate is slow. Drag to rotate. A label says "Drag to inspect".

### 4.3 In-panel hotspots

- Small pulsing markers placed on the illustration in percentage coordinates.
- On hover/focus/tap, open a "From the archive" note: the fact, plus a source link that opens in a new tab.
- Every fact must pass the `nasa-content-integrity` skill.

### 4.4 Ambient micro-animation (restrained, per mission)

- **Mars:** drifting dust particles over the dust-storm panel.
- **Apollo:** the laser beam on the "still answering" panel draws itself in, then shows a 2.5 s return pulse.
- **Ingenuity:** a rotor-blur shimmer on the thin-air panel.
- At most one ambient effect per spread. All of them pause when off-screen or under reduced motion.

### 4.5 Book → hardware transition (the payoff)

On the back cover, "Inspect the hardware" does this:
1. The 3D model appears to rise out of the final page's illustration: the mesh fades in at the same screen position and scale as the drawn hardware.
2. The book pages fall away and darken.
3. The camera dollies into the State 3 hardware diorama.
4. It runs as one continuous move of about 1.4 s, with no blank frame.
